Privacy by design

The server never sees your words.

DrafNote is built around end-to-end encryption. In plain terms: your notes are encrypted on your device before they ever leave it. Your password never leaves your device; it unlocks your notes locally.

✓

What stays private

  • Note titles, bodies, folders & sections
  • Tags, recipe ingredients & steps
  • To-do contents & progress
  • Vault notes (separate password)
  • Shared sections (only members decrypt)
∅

What the server stores

Only ciphertext + the minimum needed to operate accounts:

  • Encrypted blobs (meaningless without your key)
  • Username + login verifiers (not your password)
  • Optional email for recovery, if you add one
Seize the server and you get noise. That's the point.

Layers, not promises

🔒 Vaults

Password-protected vaults for sensitive notes, separate from your login. Lock with one click.

👥 Encrypted sharing

Shared sections invite members by username. End-to-end encrypted — new members only see what they're invited to.

📱 2FA + passkeys

Authenticator-app codes (TOTP) and device passkeys. Your password is still needed once per device to unlock notes.

🗑️ Forgiving Bin

Deleted notes rest in the Bin before permanent removal. Mistakes are recoverable.

💾 Encrypted backups

Automatic backups of the encrypted store. Export anytime as JSON, YAML, Markdown or TXT.

🚫 No tracking

No ads, no analytics beacons, no third-party fonts. This marketing site makes zero external requests.

Recovery

Only you can regain access

Even account recovery is designed so that only you can regain access to your data.

1
Generate codes while logged in

Settings → Authentication → Recovery codes. Each code unwraps your notes key and works once.

2
Store them offline

Print them, write them down, keep them with important papers — not in your notes app.

3
Recover anytime

Forgot password? Enter username + code, set a new password. Your notes stay under the same key.

Important: if you lose both your password and your recovery codes/email access, nobody — not even us — can decrypt your notes. That's zero-knowledge working as intended.
